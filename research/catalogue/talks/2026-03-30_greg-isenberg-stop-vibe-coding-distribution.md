## Summary

Greg Isenberg argues that the age of vibe coding has created a paradox: everyone can build software, so software alone is worthless. With AI collapsing 8-week development cycles into 2-day sprints, the competitive advantage has shifted entirely to distribution — the ability to reach and convert customers. His core thesis is blunt: "Distribution is now more valuable than code."

He presents seven concrete distribution strategies for the AI era. First, MCP Servers as AI Sales Teams — building Model Context Protocol servers so AI assistants like Claude and ChatGPT can discover and recommend your product automatically, achieving zero-cost customer acquisition. Second, Programmatic SEO at scale — generating 10,000+ optimized pages following "best X for Y" patterns, with math showing 300K monthly visitors yielding $60K/month at modest conversion rates. Third, Free Tool Marketing — shipping one free utility per week that markets itself through inherent value.

The remaining four strategies round out the playbook: Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) — structuring content for citation by AI search engines like Perplexity and ChatGPT, where early movers have an outsized advantage comparable to SEO in 2010; Viral Artifacts — designing shareable outputs a la Spotify Wrapped that users naturally distribute; Newsletter Acquisition — buying undermonetized niche newsletters ($5K-$20K) for instant audience access; and AI Content Repurposing — transforming one 30-minute hero recording into 20+ pieces across platforms weekly.

Isenberg emphasizes that human oversight remains essential for quality and strategic clarity, even as AI handles execution. His call to action is pragmatic: choose two of the seven strategies and start implementing them this week.
