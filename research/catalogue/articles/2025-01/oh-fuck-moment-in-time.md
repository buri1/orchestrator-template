## Summary

In January 2025, Geoffrey Huntley describes an "oh fuck" moment that crystallized his understanding of AI-assisted software development. After reading a HackerNews comment suggesting that programming languages with strong type systems and verbose compiler errors make LLMs dramatically more effective, he decided to test the thesis with a deliberately ambitious task: converting the Rust CPAL audio library to a Haskell library using GHC2024.

He instructed Cursor/Windsurfer to perform the conversion, use the Hoogle CLI to find correct types and functions, generate a comprehensive test suite using hspec and quickcheck, and build after every code change. Then he took his children to the swimming pool. When he returned, the AI had produced a fully functional Haskell library complete with autogenerated C bindings and FFI to CoreAudio.

Huntley emphasizes that this was creative work -- novel synthesis across language boundaries, not regurgitated training data. The experience led to his central thesis: software engineers who refuse to adopt AI assistants "are frankly not gonna make it." He observes that organizations are already splitting between those who embrace these tools and those who resist, with the gap accelerating.

This short but pivotal post is the origin point for Huntley's entire subsequent body of work. The back pressure insight (type systems as automated agent feedback) evolved into his full back pressure hierarchy. The "walk away" pattern became overnight multi-boxing runs. The "oh fuck" moment became the driving motivation behind his Ralph Wiggum Loop, stdlib methodology, and specs-driven development approach.
